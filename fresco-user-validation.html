Validation Plan

A structured approach to testing the Fresco marketing site and app with real users — before investing further in either.

Participants needed 5 moderated + 20 survey
Timeline 2 weeks
Documents inside Plan · Recruitment · Session guide
Part 1
01

Testing Plan

Two tracks running in parallel. Track A is moderated — you watch people use the product in real time. Track B is unmoderated — a wider group answers a short survey. Together they answer different questions.

The single most important question: Do people understand what Fresco does within 30 seconds of landing on the marketing site — without any explanation from you? If the answer is no, app quality is irrelevant.

Track A

Moderated Sessions — 5 people, 60 minutes each

Week 1 · In-person or video call · You observe, take notes, do not help

Find 5 product managers, founders, or strategists who make product decisions regularly. Not friends or colleagues who already know about Fresco. Give them nothing in advance — no description, no context. Just a calendar invite.

Who to recruit
  • Product managers at startups or scale-ups
  • Founders making build/don't-build decisions
  • Product designers who influence direction
  • Heads of Product / CPOs
  • Strategy consultants working with product teams
Who NOT to recruit
  • People who already know about Fresco
  • Friends or colleagues who will be polite
  • Pure engineers with no product decision role
  • People outside the target audience
  • Anyone who asks what it is before the session

Session structure (60 mins):

Part 1 — Marketing site 10 mins

Open frescolab.io. Say: "Take a look at this. Don't click anything yet — just tell me what you think it is and whether you'd sign up."

Let them read silently. Note what they look at, where their eyes go, when they stop. Ask: "What does this do?" Record their exact words. Then: "Who do you think this is for?" Then: "What's unclear?"

Do not explain or correct anything at this stage.

Part 2 — Live session 30 mins

Say: "I want you to use this app to work through a real decision you're currently facing. Pick something you're actually thinking about — not a hypothetical." Give them the URL. Watch without helping.

If they get completely stuck, you can say "what would you expect to happen?" — but don't tell them what to do.

Note: where they hesitate, what they re-read, what they skip, whether they read the hints, whether they look at the Analysis tab after a result appears.

Part 3 — Debrief 20 mins

"What did you get out of that?" Then: "Would you do anything differently tomorrow because of it?" Then: "Would you share this output with anyone? Who?" Then: "What would make you pay for this?"

Finish with: "How would you describe Fresco to a colleague in one sentence?" — record this verbatim. The gap between their description and yours is your messaging problem.

Track B

Remote Survey — 20 people, async

Week 2 · Via email or Typeform · No moderation needed

Record a 3-minute Loom walkthrough of a completed Fresco session — pick an Investigate session with a good result. Show the marketing site (30 seconds), then show the session from start to result (2 minutes). Send it to 20 people in your network with the survey below.

The goal of Track B is scale, not depth. You want to see whether the messaging problem you identify in Track A is widespread — or whether it only affected a few people.

Part 2
02

Recruitment Message

Two versions — one for moderated sessions, one for the remote survey. Use the moderated version for direct outreach. Use the survey version for a broader send.

Version A — Moderated session (direct outreach)

Hi [name], I'm building a product and I'm looking for 5 people to give me honest feedback. I'd love 60 minutes of your time this week or next. Here's what I need: you make product decisions regularly, and you have something you're currently trying to figure out — a direction to commit to, a problem to define, a decision to pressure-test. You'd use a real tool I'm building to work through it, while I watch and take notes. No preparation needed. I won't explain the tool beforehand — that's the point. I want to see whether it makes sense without me in the room. I'll take you through it on a video call. Completely honest feedback is what's useful — good or bad. If you're up for it, just reply and I'll send a time. Should take 60 minutes. [Your name]

Version B — Remote survey (broader send)

Hi [name], I'm working on something and I'd value your honest take. It'll take 5 minutes. I've put together a 3-minute video showing a tool I'm building for product teams — something that helps structure thinking before a major product decision. Watch it, then answer 5 quick questions. No sign-up needed. [Loom link] [Survey link] Genuinely useful feedback means telling me what you actually think, not what sounds nice. What doesn't make sense, who you think it's for, whether you'd use it — all useful. Thanks, [Your name]
Part 3
03

Session Questions

All questions to ask across the moderated session and the remote survey. Questions are ordered by priority — critical questions must be answered, important questions should be answered, nice-to-have questions if time allows.

Priority framework

Critical
Questions that directly determine whether Fresco is working
If you can only answer 3 things, answer these. Everything else is secondary.
Important
Questions that improve the product and positioning
Answer these in the debrief if you have time and they feel natural.
Optional
Questions that provide useful context
Only ask if the conversation goes there organically.

Marketing site — ask after 60 seconds of silent reading

Q1 C
What do you think this does?
The most important question. Their answer verbatim tells you whether your messaging is working. Don't prompt. Don't correct. Just listen and write it down exactly.
Q2 C
Who do you think this is for?
Tests whether your target audience is legible from the site. If they describe someone very different from your actual user, you have a targeting problem.
Q3 I
What's the first thing that caught your attention?
Tells you what's doing the most work visually and verbally. If it's not the most important thing, redesign accordingly.
Q4 I
What's unclear or confusing?
Don't ask "what's wrong" — that invites small suggestions. "Unclear or confusing" surfaces real comprehension gaps.
Q5 I
Would you sign up? What would stop you?
Not "would you pay" yet — just sign up. The barrier to a free account tells you the trust gap on the site.

During the live session — observe, don't ask

Don't interrupt. Watch and note when each of these happens.

Do they read the question hints or skip straight to the textarea? If they skip hints, the questions need to be self-explanatory.

Do they hesitate before answering any question? Which one? Hesitation = the question is unclear or asking for something they don't have.

Do they re-read the question after starting to answer? Signals the question is ambiguous.

Do they skip any question entirely? Which one? Why? (Ask after, not during.)

When the result appears, do they lean in or skim? Leaning in = the output has resonance. Skimming = it feels generic.

Do they click the Analysis tab? Do they scroll through the systems outputs? What do they stop on?

Do they want to export or share anything? Do they reach for the Export button unprompted?

Do they look satisfied or flat when they finish? The emotional response at the end of the session is the most honest signal.

Debrief — ask after the session ends

Q6 C
What did you get out of that?
Open-ended. Don't prompt. The answer tells you whether the output created genuine value or just felt like structured busywork. Silence is a valid (and important) answer.
Q7 C
Would you do anything differently tomorrow because of what you just ran through?
The real test of value. If the answer is no, the output didn't change anything. If yes, ask specifically what — that's your proof point.
Q8 C
How would you describe Fresco to a colleague in one sentence?
Record this verbatim. Run it across all 5 participants. The common words and frames are your real positioning — better than anything you'd write yourself.
Q9 I
Would you share this output with anyone? Who?
Tests whether the output has a natural sharing moment. If yes, the follow-up is "what would you change before sharing?" — tells you what's missing for it to be presentation-ready.
Q10 I
What would make you actually use this regularly?
Not "what would you improve" — that invites feature requests. "Actually use regularly" surfaces the habit or workflow change needed. The answer is usually about integration, not features.
Q11 I
What would you pay for this — and what tier would make sense for you?
Only ask this if Q6 and Q7 showed genuine value. If there was no perceived value, pricing questions are noise. If there was value, let them name a number first before mentioning your pricing.
Q12 O
What does your current process look like when you're facing this kind of decision?
Context for positioning. What is Fresco replacing or supplementing? The answer tells you the real competitive frame — it's usually "nothing structured" or "a Notion doc".
Q13 O
Is there anything you expected it to do that it didn't?
Surfaces expectation gaps set by the marketing site. If there's a consistent gap, the site is over-promising something the app doesn't deliver.

Remote survey — Track B (after watching the Loom)

Keep this to 5 questions maximum. Any longer and completion rate drops significantly.

1. In your own words, what does Fresco do?
Open text. The answer tells you whether the Loom made the product legible. Compare to Q1 from moderated sessions.
Open text
2. Who do you think this is for? Is that you?
Two-part. First part tests positioning legibility. Second part is self-qualification — tells you if the audience on the site matches people who actually feel it's relevant to them.
Open text
3. What problem does this solve — and how urgent is that problem for you?
Tests whether the pain point lands. "Urgent" is the key word — lots of people have the problem but don't feel pain about it. Pain drives buying.
Open text + 1–5 scale
4. Would you pay for this? If yes, roughly how much per month?
Only useful if Q3 showed urgency. If no one finds it urgent, pricing data is irrelevant. Let them name a number — don't anchor with your current pricing.
Yes / No / Maybe + open text
5. What's the one thing that would make you actually sign up and use this?
The most actionable question in the survey. The single biggest barrier to adoption, stated directly. Collect these across 20 responses and look for the pattern.
Open text
Part 4
04

What to Do With the Results

After running both tracks, you'll have a lot of raw data. Here's how to process it without drowning in it.

Step 1

Collect the verbatim answers to Q1 and Q8

From every participant: their answer to "what does this do?" and "how would you describe it in one sentence?" Put them in a list. Look for common words and frames. The most common language is your positioning — use it verbatim on the site.

Step 2

Identify where every session hesitated or stopped

Look at your observation notes from Part 2 of the moderated sessions. Any step where more than 2 of 5 participants hesitated, re-read, or skipped is a problem to fix. Don't debate it — fix it.

Step 3

Count the Q7 answers

"Would you do anything differently tomorrow?" If 3 or more of 5 say yes — and can say what specifically — Fresco is creating real value. If 3 or more say no or struggle to answer, the output isn't landing and that's the most important thing to fix before everything else.

Step 4

Run the results through Fresco

Use the Investigate house with what you've observed. Then Validate with the signal you've collected. Let the product analyse its own validation data. The output will tell you what to prioritise — and it's a good test of whether the product does what it's supposed to do.
